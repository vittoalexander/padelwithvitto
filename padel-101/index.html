01 / 07The court

A cage made for rallies

  1. 20 × 10 metres

    Smaller than a tennis court and fully enclosed. Always played as doubles, two against two.

  2. A low net and two service boxes

    The net is 88 cm in the middle. Service lines sit 6.95 m from the net on each side.

  3. Glass at both ends

    3 m glass back walls wrap round the corners. The rest of the sides is metal mesh. Both are part of the game.

02 / 07The serve

Underhand, and across

  1. Bounce it, then hit it at waist height or below

    Stand behind your service line. Drop the ball, let it bounce once, and swing.

  2. Send it diagonally into the box

    Start from the right side, then swap each point. The return must bounce before it's hit.

  3. Two tries, one trap

    If the serve bounces in the box and then hits the mesh, it's a fault. Hitting the glass is fine. Net and in means serve again.

03 / 07The walls

Floor first, then glass

  1. The ball must land before it hits a wall

    Your shot has to bounce on the floor on their side first. Straight into their glass loses you the point.

  2. Let the glass give it back

    After one bounce, the ball can come off the back or side walls and you can still play it. Wait for it.

  3. Send it over the net

    Hit it back before it bounces a second time. In a pinch you can even play it into your own glass to get it over.

04 / 07Scoring

Same as tennis, plus a golden point

0→15→30→40→Game

Win 6 games to take a set, by two. At 6–6 you play a tie-break to 7. Best of three sets wins the match.

40–40 is called deuce. What happens next depends on the event, so agree on it before the first serve:

  • Golden point One deciding point straight away. The most common format at clubs.
  • Silver point One advantage, then a deciding point. A club variant.
  • Star point Two advantages, then a deciding point. The official pro format since 2026.
  • Advantage Win two points in a row, however long that takes.

On every deciding point, the receiving pair picks who returns.

06 / 07Positioning

Move like you're tied together

  1. The net is where points are won

    The pair at the net attacks. The pair at the back defends and waits for a chance.

  2. Lob over them, take their place

    A good lob pushes them back. You and your partner walk up together and claim the net.

  3. Never one up, one back

    Split up and you leave a diagonal hole down the middle. Imagine a 3 m rope between you.

07 / 07Your first week

Four things before your first game

A · Racket

Round & soft

A round head has the biggest sweet spot. A softer foam core forgives off-centre hits. Leave the diamond shapes for later.

B · Shoes

Grip for sand

Most courts are artificial grass with sand on top. Padel or clay shoes with a herringbone sole stop you sliding.

C · Court

Book 90 minutes

Courts are booked by the hour for four people. 90 minutes gives you a warm-up and two short sets.

D · Coach

One lesson first

An hour with a coach on grip, ready position and the glass saves you weeks of bad habits.
